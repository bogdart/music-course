# Capstone Song One: Verse, Chorus, Bridge

This song proves Phase 3: a complete, original, multi-track song with a **bridge**, finished and mixed. Almost
everything is something you have already done, put together. One thing is new: a first, basic **mix** (levels and
pan), taught in session 2 just before you use it. **It spans two sessions:** steps 1–2
today, step 3 next time. A real song taking more than one sitting is normal.

## The brief

- Form: **Intro 4 · Verse 8 · Chorus 8 · Verse 8 · Chorus 8 · Bridge 8 · Chorus 8 · Outro 4** — 56 bars, about
  2¼ minutes at 100 BPM, in F major.
- Five tracks: drums and pad chords are ready in the template; you write the **lead**, the **bass** and one
  extra layer on **strings**.
- One **Phase 3 colour**, only one: a secondary dominant (week 24), a counter-melody or octave double (week 21),
  or a modal touch such as the ♭7 (week 22).

The template's section markers are already set. You don't need to write every bar: write one verse and one
chorus, then **copy** them where the form repeats.

| Section | Bars | Chords | Numerals | Drums in the template |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–4 | F C Dm B♭ | I V vi IV | kick + hats |
| Verse | 5–12, 21–28 | Dm B♭ F C ×2 | vi IV I V | light (verse 1), fuller (verse 2), fill in the last bar |
| Chorus | 13–20, 29–36, 45–52 | F C Dm B♭ ×2 | I V vi IV | full, crash on bar 1 (ride in the last chorus) |
| Bridge | 37–44 | Gm B♭ Dm C Gm B♭ C C | ii IV vi V ii IV V V | half-time, snare build in the last bar |
| Outro | 53–56 | B♭ C F F | IV V I I | half-time, final hit |

Notice the verse and chorus use the **same four chords in a different order** (vi first vs I first) — one of the
contrast levers from week 17. The bridge brings **ii** and ends on V, so the last chorus feels like arriving home.

## Step 1 — the lead: verse and chorus (25 min)

1. **Chorus first.** Start from a 2-bar hook (week 18): a short motif you repeat or vary. Put it at the start of
   the chorus and bring it back at least twice more. Keep the chorus **higher** than the verse.
2. **Verse.** Lower, fewer notes, more rests. It can answer the chorus hook's rhythm but should not copy it.
3. **Copy** verse → bars 21–28, chorus → bars 29–36 and 45–52. Leave the bridge empty for now.
4. **Outro:** a short piece of the hook, landing on F.

## Session 2 — bridge, extra layer, and a first mix (40 min)

**Bridge melody.** It must sound *new*: a different rhythm (long notes after a busy chorus, or the reverse), a
different register, or starting on a different degree. Let its last bars climb towards the last chorus.

**Extra layer and colour.** Add the strings where they help most — a counter-melody or an octave double of the
hook in the last chorus is the classic choice. As in week 21: at first an octave double may sound like a *new*
line rather than a thicker hook; both are fine. If you choose a secondary dominant instead, the easiest spot is
bar 43: replace C with **G7 (V/V)**, so the bridge ends G7 → C → last chorus on F. Its B natural is the ♯4 you
met in week 24. Change the pad, the bass (G) and any melody B♭ in that bar to match.

**New: a basic [[mix]] — levels, then [[pan]].** Until now every track played at the level the DAW gave it. A
*mix* balances the finished tracks so every part can be heard and the important one — the lead — is never
buried. Two controls do most of the work, and both sit at the left of each track in the arrangement:

* **Level (volume slider):** how loud the whole track is. Mixing is mostly turning things *down*: a part that is
  too loud hides the others, especially when two parts share a register (week 21's "floors").
* **Pan (the small left–right slider; double-click to re-centre):** where the track sits between the left and
  right speaker. Parts that share a register can be separated a little by panning them to different sides.
  Honest note: on laptop speakers or a single speaker, pan is hard or impossible to hear — use headphones for
  this step.

Work in this order:

1. Play just drums and bass (mute the rest); set the bass level until kick and bass feel like one low sound.
2. All drums share one track, so their balance is set by velocity (week 20's velocity lane): snare about as loud
   as the kick, hats clearly quieter.
3. Add the lead: the loudest melodic part — every note must be clear.
4. Add the pad *under* the lead: pull it down until the lead is comfortable, then a little more.
5. Strings last and lowest: you should miss them when muted, not notice them when playing.
6. Pan: kick, snare, bass and lead in the centre; pad a little left, strings a little right.

Listen at low volume: if the lead disappears, it is too quiet. Expect the balance to take a few passes; there is no single right answer, only "can I hear every part, and the lead first?"

## The finishing checklist

- [ ] The hook appears at least 3 times, and the chorus starts with it.
- [ ] Verse and chorus differ in at least two levers: register, rhythm, harmony, texture.
- [ ] The bridge brings something new, and its end leads into the last chorus.
- [ ] Every section changes at least one layer; the last chorus is the biggest.
- [ ] The ending is clear: a cadence to I.
- [ ] Mix: lead always audible, kick and bass balanced, pad and strings panned.
