# Capstone Song Two: Beat-First Dance-Pop

Song One started from chords and a hook in a major key. Song Two changes the starting point on purpose:
**beat first, A minor, 122 BPM, [[four-on-the-floor]]** (the kick on every beat, week 20). Like Song One, it
**spans two sessions**: steps 1–2 today, step 3 next time.

## One new idea: energy without fills

In Song One a drum fill announced each chorus. Dance music does it with the whole texture:

- **Build** (instead of a pre-chorus, 4 bars): the kick stops and a snare hits faster and faster — quarters,
  eighths, then sixteenths. See [[build-up]].
- **Drop** (the chorus): everything lands together on beat 1 — kick, bass, crash, hook. See [[drop]].
- **[[Breakdown]]** (the bridge): kick and bass disappear; only chords, claps and hats. The last drop then feels
  the biggest, simply because the low end comes back.

Listen to the template once, just for the energy: where does it rise, where does it empty out?

## Session 1, step 1 — the bass riff (15 min)

In house-style music the bass often plays **between** the kicks, on the "and"s — where the open hat sits in week 20's house groove — so kick and bass
never hit at the same moment. Loop this starter, then write your own one- or two-bar riff on the chord roots
and copy it through verses and drops (follow the chords: A, F, C, G). No bass in the builds or the breakdown.

## Session 1, step 2 — the drop hook and the verse (25 min)

1. **Drop hook:** short, rhythmic, repeated — two bars over Am F, answered over C G. All three drops identical.
2. **Verse melody:** lower and sparser than the hook.
3. Optional: double the hook an octave up on the **pluck** in the last two drops.
4. Breakdown: the lead rests, or plays a few long notes. Over the E chord, keep to E or B (a quick G♯ stepping into A is fine). The outro lands on A.

The melody uses **A natural minor**: the drop chords include G major (VII), and a G♯ there would clash.

## Session 2, step 3 — builds, breakdown, mix (40 min)

**Builds.** The drums already rise. Help them: a pluck or pad line that climbs through the four build bars, or
chords that get louder (raise the note velocity bar by bar).

**Breakdown.** Make it feel empty on purpose — pad, claps, maybe the hook fragment on the pluck. Then enjoy the
last drop.

**Mix a dance track.** Same level order as Song One, plus one rule: **kick and bass must not fight.** If the bass
sits on the off-beats they already take turns; if it plays with the kick, lower the bass until the kick punches
through. Kick, bass, clap and lead in the centre; pluck and pad panned a little to opposite sides.

Checklist: hook in every drop · verse lower than the drop · every build rises · breakdown has no kick or bass ·
last drop is the biggest · clear ending on Am · lead always audible, kick and bass clean, pluck and pad panned.
