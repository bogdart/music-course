# Capstone Song Two: Beat-First Dance-Pop

Song One was chord- or hook-led in a major key. Song Two deliberately changes everything: **beat-first, A minor, 122 BPM, [[four-on-the-floor]]**. Same form — I4 V8 PC4 C8 V8 PC4 C8 B8 C8 O4 — but dance music shapes energy differently:

- **Build** (pre-chorus): the kick drops out, a snare roll speeds up from 8ths to 16ths, tension rises.
- **Drop** (chorus): everything lands at once on beat 1 — kick, bass, crash, hook.
- **Breakdown** (bridge): kick and bass disappear; only chords, claps and hats. The last chorus then hits hardest.

## The template

Drums are pre-programmed for every section; the pad carries a suggested chord plan: verses and choruses **Am F C G** (i–VI–III–VII), pre-chorus **F G F G**, bridge **Dm F Am Am Dm F E E** — the E major chord is V from harmonic minor (week 9) and pulls hard back into the last drop.

## Beat-first order of work

1. **Bass riff first.** In house, the bass often plays *between* the kicks, on the off-beat "and"s — so kick and bass never collide. Try this one-bar starter, then write your own:

2. **Hook for the drop.** Short, rhythmic, repeated — often played on a pluck an octave above the lead. Write it over Am F C G.
3. **Verse melody** lower and sparser than the drop.
4. **Arrange the builds and breakdown**, then mix.

## Mixing a dance track

Same level order as Song One, with one extra rule: **kick and bass must not fight.** If the bass sits on the off-beats they already take turns; if it plays with the kick, lower the bass slightly until the kick punches through. Keep kick, bass, clap and lead centred; pan the pluck and pad slightly opposite each other.

## Checklist

- [ ] The hook appears at least 3 times, and the chorus starts with it or builds to it.
- [ ] Verse and chorus differ in at least two of: register, rhythm, harmony, texture.
- [ ] The highest melody note is in the chorus (or the bridge's peak leads into it).
- [ ] Bass plays the chord root on beat 1 of each chord (or locks with the kick in dance styles).
- [ ] Every section changes at least one layer; the last chorus is the biggest moment.
- [ ] There is a fill, build or drop before each chorus.
- [ ] The bridge brings something new: chords, rhythm, texture or register.
- [ ] The ending is clear: a cadence to I or a planned fade.
- [ ] Mix: lead always audible, kick and bass balanced, nothing clipping, parts panned.
