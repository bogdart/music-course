# Graduation 2: Transcription Test, Retrospective and Next Steps

## The transcription test

One mystery song, eight bars, everything the year has taught you: sevenths, a slash chord, a suspension resolving, a borrowed chord with a chromatic melody note, a pushed kick, anticipations. Give yourself **30 minutes**. Use the seven passes in order, fill in a form map, then answer the questions and rebuild it. Every scored part needs 80%.

## Look back

In week 1, octaves sounded like different notes to you. In week 11 you drilled hearing a chord's root. Since then you've learned every diatonic and extended chord, borrowed and secondary harmony, modes, grooves in a dozen styles, arrangement, and a method that takes any song apart in seven passes. You've written well over thirty pieces and finished a three-minute song. That's not "a beginner who did a course" — that's a musician with a working ear and a working process.

## Keep going

Skills fade without use and grow with it. A simple weekly plan that fits your three sessions:

- **Session 1 — Transcribe.** One real song per week, seven passes, a form map in your notebook. Alternate the four styles, then branch out.
- **Session 2 — Write.** One timeboxed speed song. Rotate the starting points.
- **Session 3 — Deepen.** Pick one thread: jazz standards, lyric writing, mixing, a new instrument, or singing your own toplines.
- **Every session** — keep the spaced-repetition ear review running. It's ten minutes that protect a year of work.
