# Phase 1 review

Ten weeks ago you started with the ear near zero and chords were new. Here is what you have worked on since:

- finding any note by ear and on the keyboard, across two octaves, and reading the treble clef from middle C up;
- counting and tapping rhythms in 4/4 and 3/4;
- the major scale: do to do in C, the home run, then the cadence as the reference for home;
- the same degrees in other octaves, below do, and two octaves around home — judged by function, or moved home on the keyboard;
- new keys one at a time (G, F, then D and B♭), then any key, with home taken from the cadence;
- major, minor and diminished triads, the seven chords of a key, and the four pop chords I, IV, V, vi by ear;
- phrases with half and authentic cadences, and your first 8-bar song in four layers.

Today is a check-up, not an exam: one small octave step and otherwise nothing new, pass mark 0.7, and every result simply tells the practice queue where to focus.

## How to take it

- **One sitting**, headphones, a quiet room, keyboard within reach.
- **Replay freely.** There's no penalty; real listening involves replaying.
- **Use your tools** — the keyboard, the walk home, moving a note home by 12 keys, the Reference button. That's how trained musicians work too, just faster.
- If you truly don't know, take your best guess and move on.

## Your method card

Every drill below has its **How to do it** box. Here are the same methods on one card — read it once now, and come back to it whenever a drill feels like guessing. The right column is the part that works *before* the ear does.

| Ladder | Listen for | If you can't hear it yet |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch | Up or down? Low or middle register first. | Search: play a key, higher or lower?, move, until it merges. |
| Octaves | Together: one note or two fighting? Apart: does the second note match the first "echoed" higher? | Play the first note and the key 12 above it, then replay and compare. |
| Degrees | Let the cadence finish; hold its last bass note as home. Ignore height. At rest (1 3 5) or leaning? 2 and 4 lean down, 7 leans up; 6 (la) feels like it leans down onto 5 (sol) — the app's walk home takes the short way, up through ti to do. | Find home, find the note, jump by 12 into home's octave, count scale keys up from home. Press Reference when home slips. |
| Chords | Bright, dark or tense? | Find the root, play major, minor and diminished on it in the middle octave, replay, pick the match. |
| Roots | The chord's floor — the lowest note; in bass lines, up or down from the last one? | Search the bottom note like any single note: higher or lower? move. |
| Progressions | Rest (I), lift (IV), pull (V) or sad (vi)? | Find home, then each bass note; count up from home: 1, 4, 5, 6. |
| Melody | Home first, then say the path: up/down, step/skip. Any octave counts. | One note at a time: find note 1, then each next move's direction. |
| Rhythm | Count the beat with your foot. | Tap along with the replay, counting out loud. |

## One octave step: find it anywhere

One octave rung opens today, because it pulls together everything the week-7 register work built: hear **one note**, possibly far outside your keys, and play **the same note name** in any octave. There's nothing to compare it with but your own keys.

**Try it:** play F5 (high, near the top of the drill's range, octaves 3–5), then keys around C4 one at a time, alternating with F5. Which one *blends* with it — the same colour, only lower? When you think you have it, jump by 12 up toward F5 and play both together: an octave melts. Then the same from low F3.

**If you can't hear it yet:** first find its rough height, search keys in *that* region until one merges with it, then jump by 12 keys into your hand's octave. Tried keys sound but aren't scored; **Check** answers with the last key you played.

## Reading your results

The Dashboard shows each ladder as a bar:

- **Rungs mastered, nothing behind:** solid. Reviews will keep it fresh.
- **One or two open rungs not yet mastered:** normal at this stage. The Practice page drills the skill furthest behind first.
- **Three or more open rungs behind:** the Dashboard says *practise first*. You can still continue with lessons, but give that skill a few extra short sessions.

Common weak spots after ten weeks are **degrees in other octaves**, **a new home every question**, **octaves one after the other** and **bass lines**. If one of those is yours, you're on schedule — octaves and register in particular improve over months, not weeks. What matters more than the rung number: are you using the method, or guessing? If a skill feels like guessing, go back to its right-hand column and use it on every item for a session — slow and right beats fast and random.

## Between lessons

- **Pick your focus:** on the Dashboard, the one or two bars with the most open, unmastered rungs.
- **Two or three Practice sessions of about 10 minutes** before Phase 2. In each, use the method card's right-hand column on every item you're not sure of.
- **Ready for Phase 2?** No bar has to be full. If the Dashboard says *practise first*, take one extra session on that skill; otherwise carry on — week 11 brings the bass clef, inversions and hearing the root, and the ladders keep your pace.
