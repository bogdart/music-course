# Glossary

Core terms from Phase 1 (Foundations). Other phases add their terms in `content/glossary/`.

## Arpeggio
The notes of a chord played one after another instead of together — a "broken chord". C–E–G–E–C is an arpeggio of C major.

## Bar
A group of beats with the strongest beat first (also called a measure). In 4/4 each bar holds four quarter-note beats; bar lines separate bars on the staff.

## Beat
The steady pulse underneath music — what you tap your foot or nod your head to. Rhythms are measured against it.

## Cadence
The musical "punctuation" at the end of a phrase, made by a chord movement. V→I (authentic cadence) sounds final; stopping on V (half cadence) sounds like a question. In ear drills, a short I–IV–V–I cadence is played first to set the key's home note.

## Chord
Three or more notes sounding together. The basic chord is the triad.

## Circle of fifths
All twelve keys arranged in a loop, each a perfect 5th above the last: C, G, D, A… Moving clockwise adds a sharp, anticlockwise adds a flat, and neighbouring keys share six of their seven notes.

## DAW
Digital audio workstation — software for recording, writing, arranging and playing back music on several tracks. The course has its own built-in DAW.

## Diatonic
Belonging to the current key, using only its seven scale notes. The diatonic triads of C major are C, Dm, Em, F, G, Am and B°.

## Diminished
A triad made of two minor 3rds (e.g. B–D–F), written with °. It sounds tense and unstable; in a major key it appears on degree 7 (vii°).

## Dotted note
A note followed by a dot lasts one and a half times its normal value: a dotted half = 3 beats, a dotted quarter = 1½ beats.

## Drone
A note or pair of notes (often root and 5th) held underneath a melody for a long time. It keeps "home" constantly audible.

## Eighth note
A note lasting half a beat in 4/4 (two per quarter note). Counted "1 & 2 & …". Written `8` in the app.

## Flat
The sign ♭ (typed `b`): lowers a note by one half step. B♭ is the black key just below B.

## Half note
A note lasting two beats in 4/4. Written `h` in the app.

## Half step
The smallest distance on the keyboard: from one key to the very next key, black or white (e.g. E–F, C–C♯). Also called a semitone or minor 2nd (m2).

## Interval
The distance between two notes, named by a number (counting letter names, including both ends: C–E is a 3rd) and a quality (major, minor, perfect).

## Key
The scale and home note a piece is built around, e.g. "C major". The key decides which notes and chords sound "at home".

## Key signature
The sharps or flats written at the start of each staff line, applying to every note with that letter. One sharp (F♯) = G major; one flat (B♭) = F major; none = C major.

## Layer
One part in an arrangement with its own job, such as drums, bass, chords or melody. Songs are built by stacking layers.

## Leading tone
Degree 7 of the major scale, a half step below the tonic (B in C major). It pulls strongly up to 1, which is why V→I sounds so final.

## Ledger line
A short extra line above or below the staff for notes outside it. Middle C (C4) sits on one ledger line below the treble staff.

## Major scale
The seven-note scale built from the step pattern W-W-H-W-W-W-H. C major uses only the white keys.

## Major triad
Root + major 3rd + perfect 5th (M3 then m3), e.g. C–E–G. Sounds bright and stable.

## Melody
A line of single notes, one after another, with a shape and rhythm — the part of a song you can hum.

## Meter
The repeating pattern of strong and weak beats, e.g. four-beat (4/4) or three-beat (3/4). Shown by the time signature.

## Minor triad
Root + minor 3rd + perfect 5th (m3 then M3), e.g. A–C–E. Sounds darker and heavier than major; written with "m" (Am).

## Octave
The distance from a note to the next note with the same name, 12 half steps up (C4 to C5). The upper note vibrates exactly twice as fast, so the two blend and sound like "the same note, higher".

## Perfect interval
The unison, 4th, 5th and octave. They sound especially stable and open, almost blending into one sound. P4 = 5 half steps, P5 = 7, P8 = 12.

## Phrase
A musical sentence, usually 2 or 4 bars long, ending with a cadence. Phrases often pair up as question and answer.

## Pitch
How high or low a note sounds, determined by how fast it vibrates.

## Quarter note
A note lasting one beat in 4/4. Written `q` in the app.

## Rest
A measured silence that lasts as long as the matching note value (whole, half, quarter, eighth rest). Written `r` in the app.

## Roman numeral
A label for a chord by the scale degree of its root: upper case for major (I, IV, V), lower case for minor (ii, iii, vi), ° for diminished (vii°). Numerals describe chords independently of key.

## Root
The note a chord is built on and named after. In C major (C–E–G), C is the root.

## Scale
A set of notes arranged in order of pitch that a piece mostly uses, such as the major scale.

## Scale degree
A note's position in the scale, numbered 1–7 from the tonic. Degrees describe how a note *functions* and sound the same in every key.

## Sharp
The sign ♯ (typed `#`): raises a note by one half step. F♯ is the black key just above F.

## Solfège
Singing syllables for scale degrees: do re mi fa sol la ti (1–7). A singable alternative to numbers.

## Staff
The five horizontal lines (and four spaces) on which notes are written. Higher on the staff means higher in pitch.

## Tempo
The speed of the beat, measured in beats per minute (BPM). 60 BPM = one beat per second.

## Tie
A curved line joining two notes of the same pitch so they are played once and held for their combined length.

## Time signature
Two numbers at the start of music: the top says how many beats are in a bar, the bottom which note value gets one beat (4 = quarter note). 4/4 and 3/4 are the most common.

## Tonic
Degree 1 of a key — the home note where melodies feel finished. In C major the tonic is C.

## Transpose
To move a whole melody or piece up or down by the same interval into a new key, keeping all its intervals (and degrees) the same.

## Treble clef
The curly clef (also called G clef) that marks the second staff line as G4. Used for higher-pitched parts such as the right hand of the piano.

## Triad
A three-note chord built from stacked 3rds: root, 3rd and 5th.

## Whole note
A note lasting four beats in 4/4 — a whole bar. Written `w` in the app.

## Whole step
Two half steps, skipping exactly one key (e.g. C–D, E–F♯). Also called a tone or major 2nd (M2).
